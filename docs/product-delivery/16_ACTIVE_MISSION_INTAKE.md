## Information required for each Mission

The following must be explicit before a Mission can pass the first real validation walkthrough:

1. **Value receiver and problem** — which customers, users or internal teams receive value, and what current problem is being solved?
2. **Expected measurable outcome** — what changes when the foundation is successful?
3. **Mission Owner** — one accountable human for the bounded outcome.
4. **Product / Service Owner** — enduring owner after the Mission closes.
5. **In scope / out of scope** — concrete modules, capabilities and exclusions.
6. **Current state** — what already exists, what is in progress and what is blocked?
7. **Target release or outcome date** — including milestone or phased-delivery logic where needed.
8. **Dependencies** — teams, systems, vendors, decisions and prerequisite work.
9. **Risks and approvals** — customer, operational, security, compliance and financial impact.
10. **Acceptance and closure evidence** — tests, release evidence, operational readiness, customer/user evidence and delayed measures.
11. **Next three actions** — the immediate work required to move the Mission forward.
12. **AI participation boundary** — authorized tasks, context, reviewers and prohibited actions.

## Validation finding

The candidate model did not create unnecessary information demands: the missing fields are basic accountability, scope, state and evidence that a cross-functional team would need regardless of tool or framework.

The immediate constraint is therefore **not another architecture document**. It is the absence of a concise, authoritative Mission baseline for the active work.
